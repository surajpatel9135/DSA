1. What does "reverse an array" mean?
Suppose:
Original:
[10, 20, 30, 40, 50]

After reversing:
[50, 40, 30, 20, 10]

The important point is:
Every element changes its position.

The first element goes to the last position, the second goes to the second-last position, and so on.
Think of the positions:
Original:

Index:    0    1    2    3    4
          ↓    ↓    ↓    ↓    ↓
Value:   10   20   30   40   50

Reverse:

Index:    0    1    2    3    4
          ↓    ↓    ↓    ↓    ↓
Value:   50   40   30   20   10

2. The fundamental logic
The simplest way to think about reversing is:
First  ↔ Last
Second ↔ Second-last
Third  ↔ Third-last

For our array:
10  20  30  40  50
↑                   ↑
First              Last

Swap them:
50  20  30  40  10

Then:
50  20  30  40  10
    ↑           ↑
  Second      Second-last

Swap:
50  40  30  20  10

Now the middle element 30 is already in the correct position.
So we're done.
3. Why do we use two pointers?
We use two pointers because we need to work from both ends of the array.
We call them:
left
right

Initially:
left = 0
right = arr.length - 1

For:
[10, 20, 30, 40, 50]

we have:
left                    right
 ↓                        ↓
10   20   30   40   50

The left pointer represents the position that needs to receive the value from the right.
The right pointer represents the position that needs to receive the value from the left.
4. What happens during one swap?
Suppose:
left = 0
right = 4

So:
arr[left]  = 10
arr[right] = 50

We want:
50 ... 10

In other words:
arr[left] ↔ arr[right]

But we can't safely exchange two values with only two assignments because we'd lose one of the values.
So we use a temporary variable.
Conceptually:
temp = left value
left = right value
right = temp

For our example:
temp = 10
arr[0] = 50
arr[4] = 10

Result:
50 20 30 40 10

5. Move the pointers inward
After swapping the outer elements, those positions are finished.
So we move:
left  → right
right → left

In code:
left++
right--

Now:
        left       right
          ↓         ↓
50   20   30   40   10

Then swap:
20 ↔ 40

Result:
50 40 30 20 10

Move again:
          left/right
              ↓
50 40 30 20 10

The pointers have met, so there is nothing else to swap.
6. Why do we stop when left >= right?
The condition is usually:
left < right

Why?
Because once the pointers meet:
left == right

we are looking at the middle element.
For example:
10 20 30 40 50
      ↑
     both

There is no reason to swap:
30 ↔ 30

So we stop.
For an even-sized array:
10 20 30 40
      ↑  ↑
     left right

Eventually the pointers cross.
Therefore:
Keep swapping while left < right.

7. The complete logical algorithm
Before writing code, you should be able to explain the algorithm like this:
Step 1
Start left at the first index.
left = 0

Step 2
Start right at the last index.
right = length - 1

Step 3
Compare the positions:
left < right?

Step 4
If yes, swap:
arr[left] ↔ arr[right]

Step 5
Move both pointers toward the center:
left++
right--

Step 6
Repeat until:
left >= right

Step 7
The array is reversed.
8. Think about it as a mirror
A very useful mental model is a mirror.
Original:
10  20  30  40  50

The mirror mapping is:
10 → position 4
20 → position 3
30 → position 2
40 → position 1
50 → position 0

So the position mapping is:
0 ↔ 4
1 ↔ 3
2 ↔ 2
3 ↔ 1
4 ↔ 0

In general:
index i ↔ index (n - 1 - i)

This is an important mathematical way to understand array reversal.
For an array of length 5:
i = 0
5 - 1 - 0 = 4

So:
index 0 ↔ index 4

For:
i = 1

we get:
5 - 1 - 1 = 3

So:
index 1 ↔ index 3

And:
i = 2
5 - 1 - 2 = 2

The middle element maps to itself.
9. Why not create another array?
You could create a second array:
Original: [10, 20, 30, 40, 50]

New:      [50, 40, 30, 20, 10]

But this requires additional memory.
The two-pointer approach reverses the array inside the same array:
Original array
      ↓
[10, 20, 30, 40, 50]
      ↓
swap
      ↓
[50, 20, 30, 40, 10]
      ↓
swap
      ↓
[50, 40, 30, 20, 10]

This is called an in-place reversal.
Complexity
For n elements:
Time  = O(n)
Space = O(1)

We visit roughly half the elements, but in Big-O notation that is still O(n).
10. The key concept to remember
Don't memorize the code first. Memorize this:
To reverse an array, exchange elements symmetrically from the two ends and move toward the center.

The pattern is:
FIRST ↔ LAST
   ↓
SECOND ↔ SECOND-LAST
   ↓
THIRD ↔ THIRD-LAST
   ↓
...
   ↓
CENTER

And the algorithm becomes:
left = first index
right = last index

while left < right:
    swap arr[left] and arr[right]
    left++
    right--
